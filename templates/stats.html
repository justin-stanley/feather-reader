Waiting to be polled is the backlog. It should hover near zero; a number that keeps climbing means the poller cannot keep up and feeds update less often than the target interval. Least recent poll is the worst staleness here — it reads never if any feed has never been fetched, because that is worse than any number.

Failing feeds retry on a widening delay, so they are not in the backlog — a broken feed makes the backlog look better, not worse, which is why the two are listed separately. The second figure, when shown, is feeds retrying hours apart rather than minutes. They are not beyond recovery: any of them returns to the normal interval on its next successful fetch.

Why they are failing groups those feeds by cause: fetch never got a response, status got a bad one, body could not be read, parse was not a feed this reader understands. It is there because a count on its own cannot distinguish many dead publishers from one bug here — and once, it did not: sixty feeds failed for a single reason in this reader's own code while the page said only that sixty feeds were failing.

Fetching says whether new content arrives at all. Stalled is the state to worry about: the poller is running but has not finished a round in far longer than it should, and every other number keeps looking plausible while nothing behind them moves. Reading, searching and everything already cached keep working whatever this row says.

This page is public, so it counts machines, not people: no user counts, no per-feed detail, nothing about who reads what. Failure counts are totals — how many feeds are broken, never which, never whose.
